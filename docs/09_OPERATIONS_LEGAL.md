### Compliance check of 11.11.2026 (Claude with the legal plugin, at Maxi's request)

The plugin's compliance check ran over L1 to L18, D9, U1 to U8, N1 and N2. Three research passes tried to disprove each answer against primary sources (Official Journal, gesetze-im-internet.de, Commission, DSK and state authority pages). **Result: proceed with conditions.** No built behaviour is wrong. N1 is revised, N2 gets another ground and a paper duty, D9 is more than the recorded risk, L1 and L17 grow by one point each, N3 is new. Everything else stands as answered.

- **N1 revised, Art. 50(2) AI Act.** "Nothing is built" does not hold as written. The Commission's guidelines (C(2026) 7702 final, 05.11.2026) count speech as audio in scope (point 60) and a system built in-house and put into service for own use as a provider case (points 10 and 11); an open-source speech model does not move the duty (point 74). Point 88 exempts real-time content that is consumed at once "without being recorded, stored or disseminated further", but only "when marking is not technically feasible", an objective test that does not depend on our means (point 81), and when the listener is told, which L1 does. The time until 02.12.2026 (Art. 111(4) as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) is for systems placed on the market before 02.08.2026, so not for ours. **Duty:** a measured note on whether a watermark survives the phone path, before the first real call; a machine-readable mark on stored recordings before `RECORDING_ENABLED` goes on; the same question for the callback summary the model writes and the team reads (the guidelines list "AI-generated summaries of text" as content to mark, transcriptions not). The model's text that only feeds the speech engine is an intermediate step and out of scope (point 63). Tracked in #637.
- **L1, Art. 50(1), one point more.** The greeting fits: point 37 names "explicit spoken statements at the beginning of the interaction" for telephony. Point 40 asks for the notice also where the caller is visibly "misled or confused about the AI origin", not only on the direct question (built), and calls periodic reminders likely necessary in complaints handling. **Duty:** an eval for the confused caller (built 11.11.2026, #638: the core says the truthful sentence again to a caller who asks for the agent's name or addresses it as a person, evals `eskalation_0019` and `eskalation_0020`); a reminder that an AI speaks when the complaint flow of T-10.11 starts (noted on its row in docs/07). Art. 50 adds no accessibility duty of its own (point 144).
- **N3 new, Art. 4 AI Act (AI literacy).** Amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (in force since July 2026): providers and deployers "take measures to support the development of AI literacy" of their staff, and need not "guarantee any specific level". The Commission's questions and answers ask for measures that fit the staff's knowledge and the use, and take an internal record as enough, no certificate. The team briefing (T-8.5, `docs/vorlagen/team_einweisung.md`) with its sign-off is that measure and that record; it now opens with what the AI is and where it errs. **Duty:** hold the briefing and keep the signed sheet; first real call. Maxi.
- **Supervision.** The KI-Marktüberwachungs-und-Innovationsförderungs-Gesetz is in force since 29.07.2026 (BGBl. 2026 I Nr. 223); the Bundesnetzagentur supervises (§ 2(1)) and fines breaches of Art. 50 (Art. 99(4)(g) AI Act, §§ 16 and 17 KI-MIG). No report or registration is due for an Art. 50 system (Claude's reading).
- **D9, more than a risk.** § 38(1) sentence 2 BDSG makes an officer mandatory here whatever the headcount, because the DSFA is due (L10). The authorities see a conflict of interest "regelmäßig" when the managing director is named (Hessian authority, citing C-453/21) and "insbesondere" with leading management (DSK short paper 12); Berlin fined EUR 525,000 in 2022 for an officer who supervised his own decisions, a group case. No German fine against an owner as his own officer was found. The lawful routes are an external officer (Art. 37(6)) or none at all, the second only if no DSFA were due or § 38 were repealed (no bill found). **Maxi decided again on 11.11.2026, knowing this: he stays the officer himself.** The risk is accepted and recorded; if the authority objects to the report, the route is an external officer. The report itself (Art. 37(7)) goes through the online form of the authority of the federal state of the seat; the law names no fee and no deadline.
- **N2, another ground and a paper duty.** § 312(2) no. 8 BGB (regular rounds) does not take a restaurant delivery out of the distance rules (BT-Drs. 17/12637, p. 47), so N2 stands. The ground for "no withdrawal" is § 312g(2) no. 2 (goods that spoil quickly) first; no. 9 is drafted for services on a set date (the materials name catering) and is the fallback. In the call Art. 246a § 3 EGBGB asks only for the dish, who sells and the total, which the greeting and the read-back give; the rest may sit on the website. **New:** § 312f(2) BGB asks for a confirmation of the contract on a durable medium at the latest at delivery, with the Art. 246a information unless the guest had it on a durable medium before. **Duty:** the register's receipt plus the printed delivery terms (`docs/vorlagen/lieferbedingungen.md`) go with every delivery, and with a pickup ordered by phone (the pickup case is Claude's reading of § 312c(1), not checked at a source); first real order. Maxi.

### Kitchen token stolen or leaked (security audit 02.11.2026)

When the register PC is lost or was used by someone else, the token was seen or typed somewhere it stays, or the server's `.env` left the server: whoever holds `KITCHEN_BRIDGE_TOKEN` can fetch the slips of the operation (name, address, note, dishes; no phone number) and mark them printed. Replace it at once; the slips wait in the outbox meanwhile, nothing is lost.

1. On the server: `openssl rand -hex 32`, the output becomes `KITCHEN_BRIDGE_TOKEN` in `.env` (it must differ from `AGENT_API_TOKEN`, docs/13 §3).
2. Restart `api` so it reads the new value: `docker compose -f docker-compose.yml -f deploy/docker-compose.prod.yml up -d api` (`restart` keeps the old environment). From now on the old token gets `unauthorized`, and the tablet may show „Druckbrücke meldet sich nicht" until step 4.
3. On the register PC, in Command Prompt (`cmd`), not PowerShell: `setx MAEX_KITCHEN_TOKEN "<new value>"`.
4. Sign out of Windows and in again: the task starts the bridge with the new value. Check its log (`printbridge/README.md`, step 6) and print a test order.

### Ops token leaked (#571)

Whoever holds a stack's `OPS_TOKEN` and reaches its `ops` network reads that restaurant's state (counts, times, menu status; no guest data so far). Replace it: on the server `openssl rand -hex 32` becomes `OPS_TOKEN` in the stack's `.env` (distinct from every other token, or the API does not start), then `docker compose up -d api` in the stack's folder. The dashboard gets the new value with its registry (#571). Suspected dashboard compromise: every stack's token, one by one.

### Operator phone lost, or the operator password got around (#571)

The operator login needs the password and a second factor (docs/23 §5). One lost factor: sign in with a recovery code; the dashboard shows how many are left. Every factor lost, or a takeover suspected (an unknown `operator.signed_in` or an `operator.sign_in_failed` with `reason` `factor` in `operator_audit`, which means the password is known): on the operator host, in the dashboard's container, `python -m api.dashboard.cli reset <name>`. It asks a new password twice, prints a new TOTP link and ten new recovery codes once, ends every session and forgets every device of the account; old codes open nothing. Add the link to the authenticator app at once and keep the codes offline. The reset stands in `operator_audit` as `operator.factors_reset`.

### Tablet lost, or the restaurant's password got around (#570, D34)

A signed-in tablet shows the board to whoever holds it, until it is signed out (docs/23 T8). In the stack's api container (docs/13 §3 "Access to the operations view"):

1. `python -m api.jobs.gui_access devices` lists the signed-in devices with their last use.
2. `python -m api.jobs.gui_access sign-out <the first 8 characters of its id>` ends that device at once; `--all` ends every device.
3. If the password may be known to someone who should not have it: `python -m api.jobs.gui_access link` makes a new setup link (24 hours, shown once). Setting the new password and PINs signs out every other device; give the link to the owner by a channel only they read.

Team PIN forgotten, or to be changed after staff left: `python -m api.jobs.gui_access pin` shows it, `pin --set <4 to 6 digits>` changes it; signed-in devices stay signed in and unlock with the new one. The owner's admin PIN the same way with `pin --admin` and `pin --admin --set <digits>`; it never equals the team PIN.

Every step stands in the restaurant's `audit_log` (`gui.session_ended` with the reason, `gui.setup_link_issued`, `gui.password_set`, `gui.team_pin_shown`, `gui.team_pin_set`, `gui.admin_pin_shown`, `gui.admin_pin_set`, `gui.admin_unlocked`, `gui.admin_pin_wrong`, never the PIN itself). The owner signs out one device on the tablet too: "Einstellungen" in the header, "Geräte", the admin PIN, then "Gerät abmelden".

### Requests of data subjects (U2, T-11.8)

A guest asks what we hold about them (access, Art. 15), to have it erased (Art. 17), or objects (Art. 21), by phone, mail or in person. The restaurant, the controller, answers within **one month of receipt** (Art. 12(3)); for a request on the phone line the day of the call is the day of receipt. A complex request may take two more months, and the guest is told so with the reason inside the first month. The agent has no handling of its own for such a request yet: it goes to the team like any other wish it cannot serve, so the team reads the `out_of_scope` callback cards for one.

The job runs on the server, from the stack's folder, written below as `dsr <command>`: `docker compose exec -T api python -m api.jobs.data_requests <command>`, in the role of the application, no owner needed. Exit 0 is done, 1 refused with the reason on stderr and nothing changed, 2 the tenant is unknown (`--tenant-name`, default `TENANT_NAME`). Start every line that holds a name with a space, or the shell history keeps it past every period here (Ubuntu's `HISTCONTROL=ignoreboth`).

1. **Log it the day it arrives.** `dsr log --kind erasure --channel mail --note "<the verification details as the guest gave them, every phone number they say they used>"` prints the request id (`dsr_2026_001`, also a backup label) and the deadline. `--kind` is `access`, `erasure` or `objection`, one line per kind; `--channel` is `phone`, `mail` or `in_person`; `--received-on 2026-10-30` for a day that has passed. `dsr open` lists what is unanswered by deadline (weekly routine, section 3), `dsr extend dsr_2026_001` moves the deadline of a complex request to three months, once. A line of the interim file outside the repository moves in once with `--reference` and its dates (`dsr log ... --reference dsr_2026_001 --received-on ...`, then `dsr answer dsr_2026_001 --outcome ... --answered-on ...`), without `--note` where its details are already stripped; the file goes when it is empty.
2. **Verify the person, never by the number alone.** Several guests can share one number. Ask for details only that person knows: `--name` with `--on 2026-10-12`, the day of an order or a reservation (or of the customer's last order or contact, for a guest whose orders already lost their name), or `--name` with `--street`, `--house-number` and `--postal-code`, the delivery address. The name is compared as the row carries it, with case, spaces and umlaut spelling folded: "Thomas Müller" does not open a row that says "Müller". A day opens only orders and reservations booked under the line their call came from, as the draft read it from `calls.caller_id` (`booked_on_own_line`, #523: the caller ID goes after 90 days, with an objection and with an erasure, the booking keeps its name longer; a withheld caller ID, a number the guest only named, and a booking whose caller ID was already gone when migration 027 ran open nothing), and a customer by a day or an address only with a confirmed delivery from their own line: a caller can book a draft under any name and number, so another row proves nothing (security audit 02.11.2026). A number never opens anything: rows on a second number count only with a detail of their own (`--on` again) and with the guest's word for that number. Ask the guest for every number they called from and pass each with `--phone` (repeatable): it narrows what the details open and opens nothing itself. Each detail must open one number: when a day or the address opens the name on two numbers, `dsr` refuses with exit 1 instead of joining two people. Details that open the name on two numbers between them (one day on each, or a day and the address) are refused the same way unless `--phone` names both: a namesake on the second number would be reached by the copy, the erasure and the objection (security re-audit 02.11.2026, evening). The same name on the same number reads as one person, there is nothing to tell them apart with. Asking for these details does not stop the month (an open point for Maxi); a request that cannot be verified, or that is refused, gets a written answer inside the month with the reason and the right to complain to the supervisory authority, and `--outcome not_verified` or `refused`.
3. **Read the rows on the day of receipt,** before the daily deletion job (T-11.2) strips them: `dsr find dsr_2026_001 --name "<name>" --on 2026-10-12` writes nothing and prints three parts. Soft-deleted and cancelled rows count.
   - `copy`: the verified person's rows, in the tenant only: `customers` and `addresses`, `orders` with their items under the names read back to the guest (the labels of `audit_log`, the current menu only where those are gone), `reservations`, `callbacks`, `calls`, `consent_log` by the call (no number in it) and `outbox` by the id of those rows (most payloads are thinned to their ids after their period).
   - `ask_the_guest`: what cannot be verified by details, for the team and never for the guest as it stands: a call or a callback that carries only the number, or shares its call with a second guest, and an objection on the number (it may be someone else's). `calls.caller_id` is matched on its digits, the line does not always give E.164. The team asks whether it is theirs (date, time, what it was about) and passes each one they confirm with `--claim <id>` on every later command; a call marked `recorded` holds a recording (T-11.3), which the copy names and the erasure withdraws only once it is claimed; a call or callback with only another guest's name is not offered at all.
   - `open_rows`: what stops an erasure (step 5).
   - `audit_log` holds ids, reasons and labels of the team's actions, no name, number or note: it is named in the access answer as the team's actions and left to its 12-month period.
   - outside the database, by hand: paper input slips still at the register and what the team typed into the register (D2); the call log CSV of `docs/17_ANRUFPROTOKOLL.md`, searched only when the guest names the date and hour of a call, since its verbatim phrases may hold a name; n8n executions (pruned after 168 hours, `EXECUTIONS_DATA_MAX_AGE` in `docker-compose.yml`); the router's call list and the team's phones; container logs (14 days where `deploy/journald.conf` is installed; Asterisk writes no number, docs/20 section 4). Transcripts do not exist yet; T-7.x adds them to the job with their store. `copy` names the file of a recording (T-11.3), and the team hands the audio over from the folder of the recordings.
4. **Access:** the guest gets `copy`, nothing of another guest, and the items of Art. 15(1) as the privacy notice states them (L4): purposes and legal basis (L3), recipients and processors (L5), transfers (L7), the periods of `docs/03_DATA_MODEL.md`, the source (their own call), the rights to rectification, erasure, restriction and objection, the right to complain, and the contact of the data protection officer (D9). Send it through the channel the guest names.
5. **Erasure:** first a backup, `bash scripts/backup.sh --label dsr_2026_001` (`docs/13_DEPLOYMENT.md` section 4), and `dsr find` once more: it shows what will go. Open items are closed with the guest first; `erase` refuses and names them while there is one: a confirmed reservation still ahead, an open callback, a confirmed order whose time is still ahead or whose slip the kitchen does not have, whatever its time says (waiting for "Passt", a red card, a slip on its way: "Passt" and "Nochmal senden" build the slip from these rows, allergy note included), a draft of an order or a reservation its call can still confirm (the call has not ended and the draft is younger than `max_call_seconds`, docs/04 §confirm; only time helps, at most that long, and only a reservation draft can be kept), a row whose event the dispatcher has not sent. Such an order goes off the tablet with "Passt" or "Nochmal senden"; one that will never be served goes with "Bestellung stornieren" (docs/06 §3), which needs no change in the database. A note for a slip at the register is an event like any other and holds the erasure until it printed. An event only needs time (its payload is the team's notification, `n8n/team_events.json`). A reservation or callback the guest keeps by choice is named with `--keep <id>` and stays whole until its own period. Then:
   `dsr erase dsr_2026_001 --name "<name>" --on 2026-10-12 >> ~/erasure_ledger.csv`
   In one transaction, for the verified rows only, in the tenant only:
   - the `customers` row and its `addresses` are deleted, which clears the links on its orders; an objection stays, it hangs on the number;
   - finished `reservations` (date passed, cancelled, or a draft its call can no longer confirm) are deleted with their `outbox` rows; `callbacks` get `phone` and `summary` set to `''`; `calls.caller_id` is set to null;
   - a recording of their calls is withdrawn: the next pass of the daily job, within the hour, deletes file and row (T-11.3); the answers stay in `consent_log`;
   - `orders` stay for the tax records (L18, Art. 17(3)(b)), stripped as the deletion job strips them: `customer_name` and `phone` to `''`, `note`, `customer_id` and `address_id` to null, `note` of their `order_items` to null; items, prices, times and `call_id` stay; a draft its call can no longer confirm also becomes `cancelled`, since `confirm` itself takes any draft of its call, also of one that ended, and must not send the kitchen one without its notes;
   - their `outbox` rows are thinned to their id keys as the deletion job does;
   - `consent_log` rows stay (`docs/03_DATA_MODEL.md`: the proof that the allergy question was asked, L13); they hold no number and name nobody once `caller_id` is cleared. Whether they stay after an erasure is open for Maxi;
   - `audit_log` gets `data_subject.erased` with a count per table.
   By hand: paper slips are destroyed; the register keeps its tax records (L18).
   Stdout is the erasure ledger, one line per erased or cleared row: `date,table,row_id,action`, no name and no number, the file outside the database and its backups (U7). It is written and flushed before the commit, so a row erased is always in it; stderr names `ledger_lines`, and the file has to have grown by that many. Until T-9.3 replays the ledger by itself, whoever restores a backup does so with the AI paused and `api`, `dispatcher` and `voice` stopped, and erases the ledger's rows again before the agent takes calls (`deleted` by id, `cleared` and `thinned` as listed above), so nothing erased comes back; request lines and objections newer than the dump are entered again from the mail threads. Before the answer, `psql -l` shows no `_before_restore_` copy still holding the rows (`docs/13_DEPLOYMENT.md` section 4), and the `dsr_` backup is deleted by hand once the erasure is checked.
6. **Objection:** `dsr object dsr_2026_001 --name "<name>" --on 2026-10-12 >> ~/erasure_ledger.csv` puts every number of the verified person into `recognition_objections`, so `find_customer` recognizes nobody on it (T-6.2, L12), and sets `caller_id` of their calls to null. The customer stays, an objection is no erasure. The row outlives the request log and the customer. A number still on the interim list outside the repository moves there once, and the list goes. An objection withdrawn in writing removes the row, by hand. What else an objection covers is open for Maxi.
7. **Answer and close.** Answer in writing before the deadline and say what stays and why: the order rows for the tax records without name, number or notes; the `consent_log` row; the thin line of the request log; a reservation or callback the guest kept; backups until they expire (rolling 30 days, U7); n8n executions until they expire. Then `dsr answer dsr_2026_001 --outcome done` (or `not_verified`, `refused`, `withdrawn`; `--answered-on` for a day that has passed). The daily job strips `verification_note` three months later and deletes the row at the end of the third year after; the mail thread and any file of the access copy go by hand in the monthly routine (section 3).

A pull request that adds a store updates `api/domain/customers/requests.py` and this section with it. The button of the admin view (docs/06 §4) calls the same domain code once that view exists; until then this is a server command. Open for Maxi (`docs/01_STATUS.md`, Made Assumptions): whether asking for verification details stops the month, whether `consent_log` rows stay after an erasure, and what an objection covers beyond recognition.

## 5. Security audit

A deliberate read of the code and configuration that face the outside, done at fixed occasions and not on every change, so it does not eat the usage quota (Maxi, 15.10.2026). The cheap checks run anyway: CodeQL on every pull request except those opened by Dependabot or from a fork (they are scanned on `main` after the merge, docs/13 §6 "CodeQL and dependency updates"), a Codex review on every pull request, Dependabot and the dependency review, `/security-review` in `/done` when a diff touches auth in `core/`, `telephony/`, `deploy/` or the agent token path, and `/start` reports open alerts. The audit covers what they cannot see: how the parts fit together, configuration outside Python (Asterisk, Caddy, Compose), and points that need a decision.

### When an audit is due

| Trigger | Kind | Noticed by |
|---|---|---|
| Before the first call that comes in from outside the workbench (T-10.1 step 2 against the pilot's router, at the latest before G1) | component, on that path | `/task` step 0c, `/start` step 5c |
| Before every gate from G1 on, when exposed files changed since the baseline | scoped if the baseline is younger than 30 days, else full | `/gate` step 1b |
| Before a component becomes reachable from outside the host: the production server with Caddy (T-9.2), the SIP trunk (T-10.12), a new public path behind Caddy (the operator dashboard and a customer login, docs/23 §11), n8n webhooks, a new published port in `deploy/` | component, on that component | `/task` step 0c, `/start` step 5c |
| 30 days or more since the baseline, and exposed files changed since its commit | scoped | `/start` step 5c |

Nothing else triggers an audit, and an audit never starts by itself: `/start` and `/gate` recommend it in one line and Maxi decides. A trigger that is not taken stays due and is named again at the next `/start`. A gate does not pass while an audit finding of severity P1 is open.

A task whose step makes a component reachable from outside carries the note `Security audit before go-live (docs/09 §5)` in its row in `docs/07_WORKPACKAGES.md`; its audit is recorded with the task ID in the Scope column, and holds only while no exposed file changed since its commit, uncommitted edits included, and while none of its P1 findings is open; a later change makes it due again, scoped to the files changed.

`bash scripts/audit_due.sh` does the arithmetic: it holds the exposed areas (all of `api/` except its tests, `prompts/`, the print bridge, `deploy/`, `n8n/`, the migrations, Compose, CI workflows, the backup, migration and database-role scripts; READMEs left out, the Markdown prompts kept), reads the baseline from the log below and prints the files changed since, each by name, and the verdict. `--gate` for `/gate`, `--task T-9.2` for one task with the note, `--tasks` for all open ones, judged against `origin/main` with the task list, the log and the copy of this script on `origin/main`.

Kinds of audit: **full** covers every exposed area and **scoped** every exposed file changed since the baseline; both move the baseline. **component** covers one path or component and does not move it, so a change elsewhere is never dropped from the next scoped audit.

### How it runs

- Model Opus 5.5 at `xhigh`: the audit judges the fate of a call and the outage path (`CLAUDE.md`, "Model Routing"). Read-only subagents may split the scope; every finding is checked in the code by the main session before it is recorded.
- Scoped: the files `bash scripts/audit_due.sh` lists as changed since the baseline, plus what they call. Full and component: the whole checklist on the areas or the component named.
- Findings go to the open hardening issue (#252, or its successor) as one comment: severity, file and line, scenario, proposed fix. Severity: P1 reachable from outside the host, or able to lose or misroute a call, cost money or leak secrets or personal data; P2 needs another weakness first; P3 a note. A P1 becomes its own task before the trigger that called for the audit is passed.
- One row per audit in the log below, with its kind. The baseline is the last row of kind full or scoped. The Commit column holds the commit on `main` the audit read, never a feature-branch commit: a squash merge removes that one, and the script refuses a commit that is not on `main`.

### Checklist

- SIP and Asterisk: no guest or anonymous calls; the context inbound calls land in cannot dial out (toll fraud); transfer targets are fixed, never caller-controlled; ARI, AMI and HTTP bound to loopback or the internal network, credentials from the environment; RTP range no wider than needed; TLS and SRTP on the trunk.
- Untrusted input: the caller ID is never a trust decision; limits per call (duration, turn deadline, concurrency) hold; no crafted input and no failure keeps a call from the team telephone.
- Authentication: the agent token is compared in constant time and never empty; no secret in logs, URLs or error answers; GUI and operations view only behind the proxy's authentication; state-changing requests protected against cross-site requests.
- Exposure: in production only Caddy publishes ports; Postgres, n8n and the voice service stay internal; security headers and request size limits are set.
- Data: the application's database role is not the owner; backups are encrypted; personal data in logs, outbox and call rows is minimal and deleted on schedule.
- Supply chain: requirements installed with hashes; images pinned where intended; CI workflows with least privilege, no untrusted pull request code with secrets.
- Containers: non-root users, no extra capabilities, writable mounts only where needed.
